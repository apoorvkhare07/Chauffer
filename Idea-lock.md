**The idea, in one line:** Chauffeur is a fashion assistant for Indian shoppers: discover through AI chat, personalise styling around body preferences and taste, and find useful outfits from an owned wardrobe.

**Why me (an audience that trusts me, years inside the workflow, or unique data):** The builder has already chosen and built this direction and website. A trusting fashion audience, professional styling experience or unique data has not been documented. Saathi provides practical experience building conversational assistance, not proof of fashion expertise or demand. Start with recent shopper experiences and people willing to try a real outfit decision.

## GOAL

**The one goal they hire it for:** **Life** — feel confident in how I dress and get closer to the look I wanted. Discovery, personal styling and wardrobe suggestions serve that outcome. Less browsing supports it; cheapest-price optimisation belongs after V1.

**Pain:** even after spending a lot of money and time shopping, the result may not look or feel as expected. Browsing multiple websites takes time without necessarily solving the styling problem.

**Delta 4 (steps today → steps with my product):** an illustrative nine-action journey of inspiration, repeated store searches, saved links, comparison, advice and wardrobe checking becomes four returning-user actions: describe → answer missing details → review/refine → choose/save a look. Sign-in, wardrobe setup and extra follow-ups add actions. This is an unmeasured hypothesis; PRODUCT.md contains the full journeys.

**The sin it rides (optional):** pride, expressed as confidence and personal style. Never translate this into appearance rankings, body shame or guaranteed attractiveness.

## USER

**The trigger:** needing a look for work, a wedding or the weekend and not knowing what suits me; or liking a new item but not knowing how it works with what I own.

**Today’s path, step by step:** seek inspiration → search/filter one store → repeat elsewhere → collect links/screenshots → compare → ask friends/general AI and repeat context → check my wardrobe → guess a combination or buy another piece. Sometimes give up, repeat a familiar look or feel disappointed afterwards. This is the builder’s problem account, not an interview transcript.

**Who they trust:** hypothesis: their own experience, friends with relatable taste, creators and credible product/fit evidence. Chauffeur must earn trust through useful choices, honest limits and correction. Shopper trust research remains open.

**Would they pay?** Unknown. Spending on clothes does not establish willingness to pay for styling software. Research current paid styling alternatives before using prices as evidence. Subscription pricing is undecided; no paying customers or price commitment is established.

## PRODUCT

**Onboarding:** start with a real outfit need on the existing website; preserve it through account access; allow photo/body/wardrobe setup to be skipped; show a small sourced selection with reasons. One guest preview is available before phone sign-in; continuing and private features require account access. The phone/guest work was integrated from main before the search release. Alternatively, add a few owned pieces and ask for an outfit. A portrait and full wardrobe are not required for discovery.

**The core loop:**

1. Discovery: describe a need, see supported-store options, refine and save a useful choice; complete any purchase on the store.
2. Personal styling: optionally share and correct body/proportion, fit and style preferences; receive guidance respecting them and the occasion, without fit guarantees.
3. Smart wardrobe: add/correct owned clothes, mark availability, describe the day and choose a suggested combination; review how a saved new piece might work with those clothes.

**Coming back:** a new occasion, another “nothing to wear” day or feedback after trying a look. Success means independently choosing Chauffeur for another decision; daily use is not established.

**The AI-first part:** the core loop interprets flexible needs and feedback and uses chosen style context plus confirmed wardrobe pieces to guide discovery and outfits. Merchant evidence supplies product facts; confirmed inventory supplies ownership facts. Saving data alone is not the AI value.

**V1:** AI/chat discovery, personal styling and smart wardrobe suggestions. **After V1:** in-app checkout and pricing intelligence. WhatsApp, voice-first delivery and real try-on are not required V1 loops. Existing experiments do not expand the promise. See PRODUCT.md for detailed boundaries and acceptance checks.

## MARKET

**Tailwinds (funding, search interest, timing):** hypotheses include interest in personal AI assistance, fragmented browsing and wardrobe reuse. The build demonstrates possible interactions, not market demand. No funding/Google Trends study was conducted for this sheet; do not invent market size or growth figures.

**Competitors and flows worth learning from:**

| Alternative | Current value | What Chauffeur must prove |
|---|---|---|
| Separate marketplace searches | Real inventory and merchant checkout. | Less repeated browsing and a personally useful shortlist. |
| Friends and style inspiration | Taste judgement and reassurance. | Advice responding to this shopper and their clothes. |
| General AI chat | Flexible advice and follow-ups. | Actual products and confirmed wardrobe context with less repetition. |
| Repeating an outfit or buying nothing | Avoids effort and purchase risk. | A useful new combination without forcing a purchase. |

Design references are separate from validated competitors: **Meetup** for bold readable typography, **Shop** for search and product browsing, **Relay** for centred input-led storytelling. DESIGN.md records what to take and ignore. No current paid-alternative study or competitive product trial is claimed.

**Size and fit:** close-circle count not recorded; extended-network count not recorded. Count reachable people with a recent disappointing outfit decision, an existing workaround and time/money spent. Record the groups or introductions through which they can be reached. “All Indian shoppers” is not a reachable audience, and Saathi’s family network does not establish Chauffeur’s market.

**Next validation:** the small styling check in PRODUCT.md, then a five-shopper trial recording choices, what they wore, effort and independent returns. The existing website is a starting point, not demand evidence.
